# Reading and reviewing a PR

## Keys

Not a vim user? The arrow keys work: `↑`/`↓` move, `→` opens the PR pane and `←` goes back. `?` lists every key, the line under the inbox legend reminds you of the basics, and the `<leader> menu` button at the bottom right opens every action with a click.

Navigation also follows vim: `j`/`k`, `h`/`l`, `gg`/`G`, `ctrl+d`/`ctrl+u`, `H`/`M`/`L`, `zz`, `/` with `n`/`N` (`N` searches backwards), and `:42` to go to a line. Numbers work like lazygit's: `0` focuses the inbox pane and `1` the PR pane, and `2`–`9` pick a tab inside whichever pane has focus (the inbox filters on the left; Overview, Diff and the agent's review on the right). The pane titles show every number in brackets and counts in parentheses, e.g. `[0] [2] Needs me (18)` and `[1] repo#305 [2] Overview [3] Diff [4] AI review (3)`. `l` or `enter` opens the PR pane and `h` or `esc` goes back. `+` gives the PR pane the whole width for reading an overview, a diff or an AI review, and `+` again, `0` or `h` brings the inbox back. `tab` cycles filters in the inbox and tabs in the PR pane. `ctrl+p` (or `<leader><leader>`) opens a fuzzy finder. `<leader>?` lists every key.

Everything on screen also works with the mouse (`mouse = false` turns it off). Click a PR, tab, filter or section header; click a fold head in the PR pane to open or close it; scroll either pane. In menus and pickers, a click selects a row and a second click chooses it, and a click outside closes the menu. Clicking a finding again includes or excludes it, and clicking a diff line again starts a comment on it. The leader menu's entries, confirm prompts' `[ yes ]`/`[ no ]`, the statusline's sync text (refresh) and login warning (Accounts) are clickable too. Clicks and the wheel inside the chat window go to the agent. Nothing posts to GitHub on a single click: approving still asks `[y/N]`.

Anything that writes to GitHub sits behind the leader key, written `<leader>` in these docs: `<leader>a` means press the leader, then `a`. The leader is `space` unless you change it with `leader` in the config or in the options (`<leader>,`) to `,`, `\` or `;`; prowl's own hints always show the key you chose. Press it and a menu shows what comes next, grouped into Review, Agent, Inbox, Share and prowl columns: `a` approve, `x` request changes, `m` comment, `n` a plain comment in the conversation (not a review; pending inline comments stay pending), `A` ask people or teams (`org/team`) for a review, or someone who reviewed for another look, `X` close with a comment, `D` draft or ready for review, `p` post the AI review, `c` AI reviews, `,` options. A review opens in your editor: its `event:` line takes `approve`, `request_changes`, `comment`, `close` or `draft`, where the last two post your text and then close the PR or make it a draft.

## Reviewing

Approving asks for a `y` and posts with no comment. Request changes and comment reviews open in your editor; `:wq` submits and `:cq` cancels.

The overview shows where each reviewer stands, then an Activity timeline: reviews, comments, thread replies, pushes, force-pushes and review requests, newest first, with a time column that is brighter the newer it is. A "you last looked" line separates unread activity above from what you have seen below, and opening a PR puts the cursor on the newest unread item; `]n`/`[n` jump between new items. New items are expanded and rendered as markdown; anything you have already seen (older than your last visit or your last review) starts folded to one line, as do accounts listed in `bots`. Folds work like vim: `za` toggles, `zo`/`zc` open and close, `zR`/`zM` open or close everything.

Below the reviewers, Checks lists the CI on the PR's head commit, with only the latest run of each check. A failed check stays open with what it reported: its title, its summary and its annotations (`a.go:42  want 2, got 3`). Checks that branch protection requires are marked `required` and come first, since those block the merge. Running checks show how long they have been going, and passed and skipped ones fold into one line. On a failed GitHub Actions check, `gl` shows the lines of its log leading up to the error and the step that failed, and `o` opens the check on GitHub.

Review threads appear in the overview with the code they refer to, and in the diff directly under the line they were left on. `]t`/`[t` jump between them. With the cursor on a thread, `<leader>t` opens the thread menu: `r` resolve, `u` unresolve, `c` reply.

The diff is syntax highlighted with your theme's colors. Added and removed lines get a tinted background, and when a removed line pairs with an added one the words that changed are tinted more strongly. Each hunk is highlighted as a whole, so block comments and multi-line strings come out right, and only what is on screen is highlighted.

The diff tab shows the changes since your last review when you have one, and `D` switches to the full diff. `]c`/`[c` jump between hunks and `]f`/`[f` between files. `za` folds a file. `v` marks a file as viewed, which folds it and moves on to the next file. This is GitHub's own "Viewed" checkbox, so it syncs with the web UI, and a file that changes after you viewed it is flagged again. Press `c` on a line, or select lines with `V` first, to write an inline comment. `S` does the same as a suggested change: the comment starts with a `suggestion` block holding those lines, so the author can apply your edit with a click. It is kept as pending and goes out with your next review. Each comment stays on the commit you wrote it on: if the author pushes in between, the earlier comments go out as their own review on that commit, so they land on the right lines. `dd` drops it. After a review is posted, a Slack status line for it is copied to your clipboard; `yy` copies one at any time.
